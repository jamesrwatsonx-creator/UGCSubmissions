# Agent operating contract

Read README.md and the linked operating documents at the beginning of each project. This is a brief-driven creative studio, not a generic content factory.

1. Find opportunities using the radar rules. Verify official terms; distinguish facts, inferences and unverified leads. Do not generate footage merely because a campaign has a large headline prize.
2. Present the ranked opportunities. The owner selects the brief before creative research or production spending.
3. Create a project record from templates/PROJECT_BRIEF.md. Research the client, product, audience, creative objectives, brand requirements, rights and reference content.
4. Discover relevant existing and open-source capabilities for this particular job. Inspect actual README, implementation, license, dependencies and safety before adoption. Reuse verified local tools rather than installing everything. Never execute untrusted agent skills or package scripts blindly.
5. Develop genuinely different creative concepts and opening mechanisms, not superficial variations. Match concept count to the brief; propose a shortlist of 3–5 from up to 10 distinct concepts. Compare originality, clarity, brand fit, compliance, production cost and likely judging criteria. Seek owner approval.
6. Write the script, visual beats, storyboard, audio plan, captions, production method and acceptance tests. Use the most appropriate creative and technical skills; not every video needs dialogue, a hook formula or a call to action.
7. Produce only the approved concept within the authorized budget using existing tools. Keep source, asset provenance, prompts and edit decisions traceable. Verify commercial rights and required AI disclosure. Do not represent fictional AI characters as authentic customers.
8. Inspect the rendered output and verify brief, dimensions, length, audio, captions, brand fidelity, continuity, licensing and submission conditions. Report failures and fixes honestly. Do not claim tests that were not performed.
9. Present the finished asset and submission checklist. The owner authorizes actual submission separately. Do not post, enter contests, sign contracts, buy tools or spend credits beyond authorized scope.
10. Record outcome, feedback and reusable discoveries; distinguish subjective taste from measured results. Update capabilities when proven useful; retire weak methods.

Do not commit secrets, private customer files or personal information to this public repository. Do not change repository visibility or automation settings without approval. Public docs are the baseline; exact campaign rules always take precedence if compatible with owner constraints. Report blockers explicitly.
