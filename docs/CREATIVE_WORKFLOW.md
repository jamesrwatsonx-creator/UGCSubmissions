# Brief-specific creative workflow

## 1. Understand the job
Identify the paid deliverable and what success means: judging rubric, emotional response, brand recall, conversion, explanation, humor or another client goal. Research official brand/product claims, audience, competitors, references and prohibited claims. Never fabricate a testimonial or product capability.

## 2. Activate relevant intelligence
Search current open-source repositories and skills after the brief is selected. Select for writing, narrative, persuasion, comedy, art direction, cinematography, audio, motion, brand consistency, production and QA as needed. Use the registry as a seed, not a mandatory stack. Compare alternatives, inspect licenses and code, test with a small task, document the result before adoption.

## 3. Ideate
Explore distinct concept families: visual impossibility, immediate conflict, curiosity, sensory attraction, comedy/reversal, emotional tension, transformation, demonstration, contrast, sound-first, seamless loops and perspective shifts. Original open-world gaming-inspired graphics are allowed only when the brief allows AI and commercial rights are clear; no actual gameplay or copied game assets. Include UGC-style, cinematic, luxury, animated, software explainer, motion graphics and mixed media as appropriate. Do not force an arbitrary hook on an elegant brand film.

Pitch each concept with one-sentence premise, first 1–3 seconds, narrative turn, payoff, product role, visual/audio language, duration, production risks and why the brief rewards it. Score originality, relevance, clarity, craft potential, feasibility and compliance on 1–5 scales. Owner chooses the production concept.

## 4. Write and direct
Create a timed scene-by-scene script including dialogue or VO if appropriate, on-screen language, visual action, camera, audio, transitions and product details. Prefer one strong idea over excessive scenes. Example 30-second structure: 0–3 opening, 3–10 setup, 10–22 escalation/demo, 22–27 payoff, 27–30 required brand close; adapt to brief. Re-hooks only if useful. Read dialogue aloud or measure actual audio duration. Plan rendered text separately so AI footage does not garble required copy.

## 5. Build
Choose existing OpenArt/Grok for generated imagery and motion, Claude Code/Codex to orchestrate and custom-code, OpenMontage where verified suitable, HTML/CSS/JS and motion libraries (e.g. GSAP, HyperFrames, Remotion where available and licensing permits) for precise overlays and programmatic visuals. Use approved assets, commercially licensed sound, consistent characters/product references and controlled shot continuity. Record prompts, model/tool versions, source provenance and credit usage where available. Free/open-source does not mean zero inference cost.

## 6. Quality gate
Review actual rendered footage frame-by-frame at key beats and watch end-to-end with audio. Check duration, aspect, resolution, readable safe-zone text, brand spelling/packaging, visual continuity, pacing, sound levels, no unsupported claims, no copyrighted assets without rights, AI disclosure and official submission rules. Verify required post copy separately from on-screen captions. Report unresolved defects.

## 7. Deliver and learn
Owner approval comes before external publication. Provide final asset, exact submission instructions, disclosure/caption, deadline and rights checklist. After submission, log result, feedback, cost, production time and reusable lessons. Preserve failed tests as well as wins; update the knowledge base only with evidence.
